D10.35 (2026-09-18), "think about counterparty in gov-os" — derived on the rows, no new kind needed. A counterparty is a POSITION, never a label: the other actor in an act (the actor whose operation has an actor as object/target), or the other end of a relationship. The record makes every two-sided fact structurally two-sided: one fact, two signed halves, one from each pen (design/59 Part 2a) — a single held half is a finding, not a fact, so "he said / she said" cannot exist: a dispute is two signed halves that differ, visible, never smoothed. A standing deal is a RULE binding both named actors (required acts in both directions, pattern form); performance is events compared against it; breach is the difference — so counterparty RISK is a derived reading over the other side's whole recorded history, re-derivable, never a stored score. A stranger counterparty (outside our record) is met at the border: signature verified, memory graded by the handshake, one signed receipt per crossing; their inside is unseen and their self-claims are recorded as claims (the same honest boundary as devices).
